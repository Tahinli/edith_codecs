# ec-h264 against x264, round 2: the comparison was sampling one picture a second

## What was wrong with the measurement

`bd_psnr_vs_x264` takes 24 pictures out of a real file. It took them one second
apart -- the sampler's stride was the clip's frame rate. At that spacing a P
picture has nothing to predict from, so the encoder was being measured on
content it never sees in a real encode. The give-away is the macroblock mix:
on the film clip our P slices went **93.2% intra, 6.3% skip, 0.5% coded inter**.
Every P-path constant in the crate had been swept against that.

The sampler now takes consecutive pictures. `EC_H264_CLIP_STRIDE` restores a
wider spacing for the deliberately-uncorrelated case, and `EC_H264_CLIP_SKIP`
passes over a film's titles and fades before sampling.

Baselines move a long way once the pictures are correlated:

| clip                | one a second | consecutive |
| ------------------- | -----------: | ----------: |
| film 3840x1608      |       -0.110 |      -1.521 |
| screen capture 2560x1440 | -0.977 |      -1.904 |

The honest number was worse than the one that had been reported.

## What the corrected measurement then showed

At every QP we were spending 20-42% more bits than x264 for a similar PSNR, and
the premium *grew* with QP. Attribution, by disabling x264's own features on
the screen capture:

| x264 running                       | BD-PSNR ours vs x264 |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------: |
| as configured                      |               -0.977 |
| `trellis=0`                        |               -0.773 |
| `trellis=0:subme=1:me=dia`         |               -0.203 |

So roughly 0.2 dB of x264's lead is its rate-distortion quantisation and 0.57 dB
is its motion search. (Those three rows are on the old sampler; they were what
pointed at the search.)

## Two fixes

**The intra side of the mode decision signalled nothing.** The inter side pays
for its motion vector; going intra pays for a macroblock type and sixteen
prediction modes, and the SATD of an intra prediction says nothing about them.
A penalty had been swept before and landed at zero -- on the one-a-second
content, where it had nothing to suppress. Re-swept on consecutive pictures:

| bits | film   | screen | synthetic |
| ---: | -----: | -----: | --------: |
|    0 | -1.521 | -1.904 |    -5.718 |
|    4 | -1.305 | -1.290 |    -5.400 |
|    8 | -1.223 | -0.920 |    -5.290 |
|   12 | -1.211 | -0.733 |    -5.286 |
|   16 | -1.267 | -0.650 |    -5.262 |
|   32 | -1.649 | -0.586 |        -- |

Screen capture keeps improving to 48 and film falls off after 16, so the landed
value is 12 -- the overlap of the two flat parts, not either optimum.

**Sub-sample refinement ran on SAD.** Interpolation smooths, and a sum of
absolute differences reads the smoothing as a better match. The refinement now
costs candidates on SATD, with the whole-sample winner re-costed in the same
domain first: +0.057 dB on film, +0.037 on screen capture, +0.74 on the
synthetic clip.

## Result

| clip           | before | after  |
| -------------- | -----: | -----: |
| film           | -1.521 | -1.154 |
| screen capture | -1.904 | -0.696 |
| synthetic      | -5.718 | -4.542 |

The bit premium is also flat in QP again rather than growing: film +14-16% at
every QP, screen capture +17-18%, where before it ran +23% at QP 22 to +42% at
QP 34.

The synthetic floor moves with it, -5.75 -> -4.6.

## Constants re-swept under the corrected sampler

The two contents are quoted film / screen capture.

- **Intra_4x4 in P pictures**: on reads -1.154 / -0.696, off reads -1.989 /
  -0.564. The contents disagree in sign; film's margin is three times screen
  capture's, so it stays on.
- **P_Skip rebate**: 0 and 2 read -1.102 / -0.741 and -1.154 / -0.696 -- the
  contents disagree by less than the step, so 2 stays. Six costs 0.14 dB,
  twelve costs 0.42.
- **Intra_4x4 discount on the 16x16 pre-cost**: 0.65/0.75/0.85/1.0 read
  -1.216/-1.154/-1.182/-1.373 on film and -0.970/-0.696/-0.591/-0.562 on screen
  capture. Three quarters is film's optimum and inside screen capture's flat
  part, so it stays.

## What is still open

x264's rate-distortion quantisation (trellis) is worth about 0.2 dB and we have
none. The remaining bit premium is flat in QP, which is the shape of a coder
that codes the same decisions less cheaply rather than one making worse
decisions.

## Addendum: two parked decisions rested on the old sampler

Both were re-measured on consecutive pictures.

**`Preset::Balanced`.** It had read -1.587 dB where `Fast` read -1.557 -- worse,
for 40% more time, which is why dropping it was on the table. On correlated
pictures it wins on both contents: film -0.875 against Fast's -1.154, screen
capture -0.506 against -0.696, for 11% more wall clock on the whole comparison.
Its wider search range and half-macroblock partitions had nothing to find when
consecutive pictures were a second apart. The question is withdrawn.

**`transform_8x8`.** It had read +0.827 dB on natural 720p and 0.000 on the
screen capture. Re-measured: **+0.800 dB on the film clip and +0.537 on the
screen capture**, at an 8x8 macroblock share of 17.3% and 14.2%. It is now a
win on both contents; what is still owed is the decision to flip the default,
because that changes every default stream byte and re-pins the byte-identity
tests.

## Addendum: where the rest of the gap is

With x264's own features disabled one at a time, on consecutive pictures
(ours reads -1.154 film / -0.696 screen capture):

| x264 running     |   film |  screen |
| ---------------- | -----: | ------: |
| as configured    | -1.154 |  -0.696 |
| `trellis=0`      | -0.959 |  -0.594 |
| `me=dia`         | -1.115 |  -0.696 |
| `subme=4`        | -0.905 |  -0.579 |
| `subme=1`        | -0.324 |  -0.244 |

The whole-sample search pattern is worth 0.04 dB and 0.00; rate-distortion
quantisation is worth 0.20 and 0.10; **sub-sample and mode refinement is worth
0.83 and 0.45** -- almost the entire remaining gap. x264's `subme` 6 and 7 are
its rate-distortion mode decision, which is the named next lane.

Iterating our own sub-sample refinement to convergence at `Fast` (it stopped
after one pass) is the first 0.111 dB of that, for 2% of the comparison's wall
clock: film -1.154 -> -1.043, screen capture -0.696 -> -0.661, synthetic
-4.542 -> -4.449.

The search's motion-vector weight was swept with it and held at a half:
0.25/0.5/1.0/2.0 read -1.074/-1.043/-1.225/-1.618 on film and
-0.676/-0.661/-0.795/-1.201 on screen capture. Charging a vector's whole
signalling cost is worse than charging half on both contents; it now says so in
a named constant rather than a bare `/ 2.0`.

## Addendum: the intra-versus-inter decision on real coded cost

The attribution in this report put 0.83 dB (film) and 0.45 dB (screen capture)
behind x264's sub-sample and mode refinement, and the sub-sample half landed in
the previous slice. This is the mode half: the intra-versus-inter decision now
codes both branches for real and keeps the one with the smaller squared error
plus lambda times its actual bits, which is what x264 does from `subme 6` up.

What it replaced compared an Intra_16x16 SATD, discounted by a fixed 3/4 for
the Intra_4x4 that usually follows, against a coded inter cost, and paid for
the intra side's signalling with a guessed twelve bits. Three approximations
where the encoder can measure instead.

BD-PSNR against x264 at GOP 10 over QP 22/26/30/34, both real clips sampled
consecutively from picture 1500:

|                    | film 3840x1608 | screen 2560x1440 | synthetic 352x288 |
|--------------------|----------------|------------------|-------------------|
| SATD and a guess   | -1.043         | -1.064           | -4.449            |
| real coded cost    | **-0.728**     | **-0.500**       | -4.523            |

The bit premium over x264 falls with the distance rather than being traded for
it: the screen capture went from +34.4% to +17.2% bits at QP 22 and from +15.9%
to +9.7% at QP 34; the film clip from +12.8% to +12.2% at QP 22.

Cost: each P macroblock is coded twice and the loser's reconstruction and
entropy writer are thrown away. The screen-capture measurement run went 28.8 s
to 30.8 s wall, x264 and the ffmpeg decodes included.

### Lambda in that decision was swept, not assumed

| lambda scale | film   | screen | synthetic |
|--------------|--------|--------|-----------|
| 0.5          | --     | --     | -5.398    |
| 0.7          | --     | --     | -4.872    |
| **1.0**      | -0.728 | -0.500 | -4.523    |
| 1.4          | -0.744 | -0.477 | -4.310    |
| 2.0          | -0.778 | -0.483 | -4.300    |
| 3.0          | -0.835 | -0.494 | --        |

The two real clips are flat between 1.0 and 1.4 and fall off above it; the
synthetic clip keeps improving to 2.0. Unscaled is the film optimum, within
0.023 dB of the screen capture's, and it puts no unmeasured constant in front
of the standard multiplier. The synthetic clip's 0.074 dB is the price, and the
floor in `bd_psnr_vs_x264` moved -4.5 to -4.6 to hold the new number.

### Constants re-swept under the new decision

`allow_i4` in P pictures: on everywhere still. Restricting Intra_4x4 to I
pictures reads -1.613 (film) and -0.508 (screen capture) against -0.728 and
-0.500 with it on. Under the old decision the screen capture preferred the
restriction by a third of a dB; coding both branches for real removed that
disagreement, which is the same class of finding as the sampler: a constant
tuned against a proxy measurement inverts when the proxy goes away.

Two constants of the old decision are gone rather than re-swept, because
nothing reads them any more: the twelve-bit intra signalling estimate and the
3/4 Intra_4x4 discount.

### What is left

Rate-distortion quantisation (x264's trellis) is worth 0.20 dB on film and 0.10
on the screen capture by the ablation in this report, and we still have none.
That is the next slice.

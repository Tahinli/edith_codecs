# ec-h264 against x264, round 2: the comparison was sampling one picture a second

## What was wrong with the measurement

`bd_psnr_vs_x264` takes 24 pictures out of a real file. It took them one second
apart -- the sampler's stride was the clip's frame rate. At that spacing a P
picture has nothing to predict from, so the encoder was being measured on
content it never sees in a real encode. The give-away is the macroblock mix:
on the film clip our P slices went **93.2% intra, 6.3% skip, 0.5% coded inter**.
Every P-path constant in the crate had been swept against that.

The sampler now takes consecutive pictures. `EC_H264_CLIP_STRIDE` restores a
wider spacing for the deliberately-uncorrelated case, and `EC_H264_CLIP_SKIP`
passes over a film's titles and fades before sampling.

Baselines move a long way once the pictures are correlated:

| clip                | one a second | consecutive |
| ------------------- | -----------: | ----------: |
| film 3840x1608      |       -0.110 |      -1.521 |
| screen capture 2560x1440 | -0.977 |      -1.904 |

The honest number was worse than the one that had been reported.

## What the corrected measurement then showed

At every QP we were spending 20-42% more bits than x264 for a similar PSNR, and
the premium *grew* with QP. Attribution, by disabling x264's own features on
the screen capture:

| x264 running                       | BD-PSNR ours vs x264 |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------: |
| as configured                      |               -0.977 |
| `trellis=0`                        |               -0.773 |
| `trellis=0:subme=1:me=dia`         |               -0.203 |

So roughly 0.2 dB of x264's lead is its rate-distortion quantisation and 0.57 dB
is its motion search. (Those three rows are on the old sampler; they were what
pointed at the search.)

## Two fixes

**The intra side of the mode decision signalled nothing.** The inter side pays
for its motion vector; going intra pays for a macroblock type and sixteen
prediction modes, and the SATD of an intra prediction says nothing about them.
A penalty had been swept before and landed at zero -- on the one-a-second
content, where it had nothing to suppress. Re-swept on consecutive pictures:

| bits | film   | screen | synthetic |
| ---: | -----: | -----: | --------: |
|    0 | -1.521 | -1.904 |    -5.718 |
|    4 | -1.305 | -1.290 |    -5.400 |
|    8 | -1.223 | -0.920 |    -5.290 |
|   12 | -1.211 | -0.733 |    -5.286 |
|   16 | -1.267 | -0.650 |    -5.262 |
|   32 | -1.649 | -0.586 |        -- |

Screen capture keeps improving to 48 and film falls off after 16, so the landed
value is 12 -- the overlap of the two flat parts, not either optimum.

**Sub-sample refinement ran on SAD.** Interpolation smooths, and a sum of
absolute differences reads the smoothing as a better match. The refinement now
costs candidates on SATD, with the whole-sample winner re-costed in the same
domain first: +0.057 dB on film, +0.037 on screen capture, +0.74 on the
synthetic clip.

## What is still open

x264's rate-distortion quantisation (trellis) is worth about 0.2 dB and we have
none. The remaining bit premium is flat in QP, which is the shape of a coder
that codes the same decisions less cheaply rather than one making worse
decisions.

## Addendum: the intra-versus-inter decision on real coded cost

The attribution in this report put 0.83 dB (film) and 0.45 dB (screen capture)
behind x264's sub-sample and mode refinement, and the sub-sample half landed in
the previous slice. This is the mode half: the intra-versus-inter decision now
codes both branches for real and keeps the one with the smaller squared error
plus lambda times its actual bits, which is what x264 does from `subme 6` up.

What it replaced compared an Intra_16x16 SATD, discounted by a fixed 3/4 for
the Intra_4x4 that usually follows, against a coded inter cost, and paid for
the intra side's signalling with a guessed twelve bits. Three approximations
where the encoder can measure instead.

BD-PSNR against x264 at GOP 10 over QP 22/26/30/34, both real clips sampled
consecutively from picture 1500:

|                    | film 3840x1608 | screen 2560x1440 | synthetic 352x288 |
|--------------------|----------------|------------------|-------------------|
| SATD and a guess   | -1.043         | -1.064           | -4.449            |
| real coded cost    | **-0.728**     | **-0.500**       | -4.523            |

The bit premium over x264 falls with the distance rather than being traded for
it: the screen capture went from +34.4% to +17.2% bits at QP 22 and from +15.9%
to +9.7% at QP 34; the film clip from +12.8% to +12.2% at QP 22.

Cost: each P macroblock is coded twice and the loser's reconstruction and
entropy writer are thrown away. The screen-capture measurement run went 28.8 s
to 30.8 s wall, x264 and the ffmpeg decodes included.

## Addendum: rate-distortion quantisation, attempted and not landed

x264's trellis is still worth what the ablation said after the mode decision
changed -- re-measured, because a stale premise is the failure mode this lane
has already hit twice. Ours against x264 with `trellis=0` reads -0.535 dB on
the film clip and -0.270 on the screen capture, against -0.728 and -0.478 with
x264 at its default: 0.193 and 0.208 dB of real headroom.

A coefficient-level walk-back was built and measured against it. For each inter
luma 4x4 block, levels are stepped toward zero from the end of the scan while
squared error plus lambda times bits falls; the bit estimate was made
scan-position aware, since a significance flag is only sent up to the last
non-zero level and that saving is the whole reason the technique pays. Then the
whole macroblock was coded once per candidate strength and the winner picked on
*exact* coded bits, using the trial machinery the mode decision already has.

It buys about 0.02 dB, an order of magnitude short:

| candidates (multiples of the zero-block lambda) | synthetic | film   | screen |
|-------------------------------------------------|-----------|--------|--------|
| none (landed)                                    | -4.523    | -0.728 | -0.478 |
| 0, 0.5                                           | -4.523    | --     | --     |
| 0, 1                                             | -4.512    | -0.728 | -0.480 |
| 0, 2                                             | -4.520    | --     | --     |
| 0, 1, 2, 4                                       | -4.563    | --     | --     |
| 0, 4                                             | -4.538    | --     | --     |
| 0, 8                                             | -4.564    | --     | --     |

Two findings, both of which the next attempt has to answer:

  * **A superset of candidates chosen on exact cost measured worse.** Adding 2
    and 4 to the candidate set can only lower the local cost of every
    macroblock, and it lost 0.05 dB against the pair. So the local cost is not
    the quantity the BD-PSNR measures, and searching it harder finds a worse
    answer. The obvious explanation -- that a reference picture's quality is
    worth more than the local metric can see, GOP 10 making every P picture a
    reference for nine others -- does not survive a check: x264 reports
    `mbtree=0 rc=cqp` in the streams this test compares against, so it wins
    these 0.2 dB with no propagation term either. What is left is that the
    candidates are generated against an estimate and only *chosen* on exact
    bits, and that choosing greedily per macroblock changes the CABAC contexts
    the next macroblock is measured in. Which of those two it is has not been
    measured.
  * **The candidate generator is rate-blind where it matters.** The levels are
    walked using an estimate; exact bits only pick between finished
    macroblocks. x264 decides each coefficient against its actual CABAC
    context cost, which is the difference the numbers above are consistent
    with.

So the machinery is not landed -- it doubles the inter coding work for 0.02 dB.
The next attempt should decide levels where the contexts exist, at write time,
which inter luma allows because its reconstruction does not feed the next
block's prediction the way intra's does; and it needs a term for what a
reference picture's quality is worth to the pictures that predict from it,
without which more search measures worse.

## Rate-distortion quantisation, landed on the coder's own price

The attempt above failed on an estimate. This one prices bins with the CABAC
engine itself and lands 0.100 dB on the film clip, 0.029 on the screen capture
and 0.450 on the synthetic gate, for no measurable encode time.

### The price is the thing itself

`CabacEnc::residual_block_cost` runs the bins `residual_block` would write --
the same code, through a `BinSink` the two share -- against a copy of the
context state, charging each bin `-log2(p)` for its state from a 128-entry
table and moving the state exactly as the coder would. So a level dropped is
priced with the significance map that follows from it, and the adaptation
inside the block is priced too.

Its accuracy is asserted, not assumed: over 400 random blocks it predicts
2 649 288/256 bits where the arithmetic coder then spends 2 650 112/256, an
error of 0.03% (`residual_cost_predicts_the_bits_spent`). A wrong price would
be invisible in the stream -- it would only show up as lost dB -- which is why
it is a test and not a comment.

### What actually moved the numbers

Three changes, measured one at a time against film -0.728, screen -0.500,
synthetic -4.523:

| change                                        | synthetic | film   | screen |
|-----------------------------------------------|-----------|--------|--------|
| levels walked down on exact cost              | -4.530    | -0.732 | -0.449 |
| + dead zone 1/6 -> 1/2 for the walked path    | -4.229    | -0.630 | -0.496 |
| + whole-block zero decided on exact cost      | -4.087    | -0.628 | -0.469 |
| + search bounded to levels <= 2               | -4.073    | -0.628 | -0.471 |

The first row is the honest answer to the previous attempt's question: an exact
rate on its own buys 0.05 dB on screen content and nothing on film. **The dead
zone was the missing half.** A search that can only walk levels *down* cannot
see a level the quantiser's 1/6 dead zone already threw away, so it was
choosing among the survivors of a decision it was meant to replace. Quantising
the searched path at 1/2 -- round to nearest, x264's own arrangement when
trellis is on -- and letting the search take the levels back down is worth
0.102 dB on film and 0.301 on the synthetic clip.

The class: **a search bounded by the heuristic it replaces measures the
heuristic.** The dead zone constant was swept and defended (`enc/quant.rs`
says the inter offset is inert across 6/64 to 32/64), and that was true --
of a path with no search after it.

The two real clips disagree about the rate term -- film wants it lighter,
screen capture heavier -- and 1.0 is the crossing point that costs neither.
The 1.25 and 1.5 rows are what a screen-only tuning would take, at 0.03 to

Bounding the search to levels of 2 or less (`RDOQ_MAX_LEVEL`) costs nothing and
is what makes it free: unbounded, the search tripled our encode time on the

carrying real signal and never wins by shrinking. 1, 2 and 3 read

best of the three on the clip that gains least.

### What is not covered

Intra blocks stay out: their reconstruction is what the next block in the
macroblock predicts from, so a level changed there moves a prediction the
decision cannot see. Inter chroma AC and the 8x8 transform path are untouched
and are the next place to look -- x264's trellis covers both. The
coded_block_flag context the price is taken against assumes both neighbours
carry coefficients rather than reading them, which is a constant shift across
candidates except for the whole-block-zero one.

x264's own trellis is worth 0.193 dB on the film clip and 0.208 on the screen
capture (measured by ablation, r2 above). We now take 0.100 and 0.029 of that.

## The same price, applied to inter chroma AC

The luma decision was extended to the chroma AC blocks: they are quantised,
searched against the CABAC coder's own price for their levels, and given the
same exact whole-block-zero test, through the one `rdoq_4x4` the luma path
uses (it now takes the block category and the DC-skipping flag).

The dead zone had to be re-swept there, because the class the luma work named
says a one-directional search measures whatever rounding feeds it. Chroma
answered the opposite way. BD-PSNR against x264, at the settled luma
constants:

| chroma rounding under the search | film 3840x1608 | screen capture 2560x1440 |
|---|---|---|
| no chroma search (baseline)      | -0.628 | -0.471 |
| 1/2 (what luma wanted)           | -0.653 | -0.482 |
| 1/3                              | -0.644 | -0.481 |
| 1/4                              | -0.623 | -0.472 |
| 1/6 (the inter dead zone it had) | **-0.593** | **-0.468** |

So chroma keeps its dead zone and takes only the pruning half of the search.
Chroma AC is sparse at these quantisers: offering more levels spends more bits
than the search wins back, and every widening is monotonically worse. The
synthetic gate does not move (-4.073 either way) -- its chroma planes are flat.

Landed: film -0.628 -> -0.593 (0.035 dB), screen capture -0.471 -> -0.468.
x264's trellis is worth 0.193 dB on the film clip and 0.208 on the screen
capture, so the two slices together take 0.135 and 0.032 of it.

The 8x8 luma transform path is still untouched.

## The same price, applied to the 8x8 transform path

`code_block_8x8` now quantises, searches and zero-tests its inter blocks the
way the 4x4 luma path does, on a `residual_block_8x8_cost` that shares its
bins with the writer (asserted to 1% over 200 random 64-coefficient blocks,
`residual_8x8_cost_predicts_the_bits_spent`).

The x264 comparison cannot see this path -- both sides run with `8x8dct=0` at
matched features -- so it is measured as BD-PSNR of the opt-in `transform_8x8`
flag against flag-off, which isolates it: the flag-off curve does not go
through an 8x8 block.

| rounding under the search | film 3840x1608 | screen capture 2560x1440 |
|---|---|---|
| no search (baseline)  | +0.535 | +0.042 |
| 1/6                   | +0.551 | -- |
| 1/3                   | +0.605 | +0.075 |
| **1/2**               | **+0.619** | +0.067 |

8x8 answers the way 4x4 luma did and chroma did not: its blocks carry enough
coefficients for the wider rounding to pay for itself. Landed at 1/2 --
film +0.535 -> +0.619 (0.084 dB), screen capture +0.042 -> +0.067. The average

`transform_8x8` is still opt-in, so this changes no default stream byte; the
open decision to flip that default is worth 0.084 dB more than it was.

ones that decide what to spend them on.

## Where the bits actually go

The BD-PSNR gate says how much we overspend. It cannot say on what. So the decoder
now counts, per syntax class, the CABAC bits a stream spends: `BitAccount` marks the
arithmetic decoder's position (`bit_position() * 256 + range.leading_zeros()`) before
and after each syntax element and attributes the difference to one of eight classes.
Because the counting lives in the decoder, our stream and x264's stream are measured
by the same instrument on the same sources at the same QP -- `bit_account_vs_x264`
in `tests/encode.rs` encodes both, decodes both, and prints the table.

Twelve consecutive pictures, GOP 10, CABAC, our defaults against the matched x264
feature set. Percentages are ours relative to x264.

3840x1608 film clip:

| mb_type | -11.2% | +3.7% |

| cbp+qp | +6.6% | +25.5% |
| intra modes | +11.2% | +12.5% |
| residual luma | +19.8% | +23.9% |
| residual chroma | +7.8% | +6.9% |
| total accounted | +10.9% | +13.4% |

| --- | --- | --- |

| mb_type | -12.2% | +0.8% |

One shape in all three sources. We spend *less* than x264 on describing motion --
fewer skip bits, fewer mb_type bits, fewer mvd bits -- and more on everything that
follows from the prediction those choices produce: residual luma first, then the
coded-block-pattern and QP deltas that carry it, then the intra modes we fall back
to when inter prediction is not good enough. x264 buys a cheaper residual by paying
more for the motion field, and comes out ahead on the sum.

That is a prediction result, not an entropy-coding one. The residual coder is now
priced exactly and quantised against that price; the next thing to look at is what
we hand it -- how the motion search chooses, and how the partition and mode decision
weighs a more expensive motion field against the residual it saves.

## The two gates on the motion search, swept

The bit account says the residual is where we overspend and the motion field is
where we underspend, so the first thing to check is whether the search is being
stopped early. Two constants decided that, and neither had been measured.

The early exit: a partition whose best seed already cost less than half a level
a sample was accepted without refinement. Swept, in levels a sample:

| threshold | film | screen capture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 (no exit) | **-0.586** | **-0.466** |
| 0.25 | -0.587 | -0.470 |
| 0.5 (was) | -0.593 | -0.468 |
| 1.0 | -0.617 | -0.476 |

Monotone in the direction of searching more, and the encode-side suite does not
measure the extra work (71.7 s against 71.8 s), so the exit is gone. A seed that
already fits is exactly the case where one more refinement step is cheap.

The split gate, which decides whether the 16x8 and 8x16 halves are searched at
all, reads identically at 0, 0.5, 1 and 2 levels a sample on both clips -- real
content clears it everywhere, so the halves are always searched today. It stays
as a guard for flat content, now documented as one rather than as a tuned value.

Small numbers, and that is the finding: the search's stopping rules are not what
holds the motion field back. What we do not have is the shape -- the encoder
codes 16x16, 16x8 and 8x16 and nothing smaller, while x264 has the 8x8 sub-
partitions. That is the next slice.

## P_8x8, and the preset that hid it

The encoder coded 16x16, 16x8 and 8x16 and nothing smaller. It now codes P_8x8
with four P_L0_8x8 sub-partitions: `PShape::Quad`, a `sub_mb_type` writer on
both entropy coders, and four searches seeded from the whole-macroblock winner.

Landing it exposed a defect in the comparison itself. The first measurement of
P_8x8 moved neither clip by a thousandth of a decibel, and neither had the split
gate swept just before it. A temporary shape histogram said why: over 1,200,000
macroblocks of the screen capture, `whole=1200000 horiz=0 vert=0 quad=0`. The
partition search runs only at `Preset::Balanced`, and `bd_psnr_vs_x264` runs
`Preset::Fast` unless told otherwise -- so every partition number taken through
the default gate was flat by construction. The histogram stays in the encoder
behind `EC_H264_SHAPE_STATS`, because a flat sweep and a dead code path read
the same from a rate curve and only a count separates them.

Measured where the shapes are actually searched (`EC_H264_PRESET_BALANCED=1`,
consecutive pictures, GOP 10, BD-PSNR against x264):

| P_8x8 charged | film | screen capture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| not coded | -0.382 | -0.369 |
| 6 bits | -0.379 | -0.364 |
| 12 bits | **-0.377** | **-0.353** |
| 20 bits | -0.378 | -0.356 |

Both contents put the optimum at twelve bits, which is about what P_8x8 signals:
an mb_type one bin longer than 16x8's plus four sub_mb_type bins. Quarters are
worth 0.005 dB on camera content and 0.016 on screen capture.

`balanced_partitions_decode_bit_exactly` now encodes at Balanced under both
entropy coders and requires ffmpeg to reproduce our reconstruction sample for
sample, and requires the sweep to have coded at least one of every shape -- the
existing bit-exactness sweeps run the default preset, so no `sub_mb_type` had
ever been put in front of another decoder before this.

The decoder counts shapes as well as bits now, so the mix is comparable too.
Screen capture at qp 26, twelve pictures, both encoders at their fullest search:

| | skip | 16x16 | 16x8 | 8x16 | 8x8 | intra |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ours | 134847 | 4792 | 488 | 226 | 103 | 32344 |
| x264 | 132159 | 6410 | 920 | 355 | 294 | 32662 |

We still split about half as often as x264 at every size, and residual luma is
still +10.9%. The gain from charging P_8x8 *more* rather than less says where
that comes from: each sub-partition is searched against the whole macroblock's
motion predictor, not its own, so the search misjudges what a split costs to
signal and picks the wrong ones. Deriving the real predictor inside the search
is the next slice.

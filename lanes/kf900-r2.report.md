## 3. Chroma residue -- root cause found and fixed (one of at least two)

`decode.rs`, `Neighbours::smooth_uv_neighbour` (now at :4640): added
`let (mi_r, mi_c) = (mi_r & !1, mi_c & !1);`.

libaom `set_mi_row_col` (`blockd.h`) snaps to the chroma REFERENCE block first --
`base_mi = &xd->mi[-(mi_row & ss_y) * stride - (mi_col & ss_x)]` -- and only then takes
`chroma_above_mbmi = base_mi[-stride]` / `chroma_left_mbmi = base_mi[-1]`. A sub-8 pair codes
its chroma at the ODD mi, so we looked one mi row/column too far, the mi-exact slot never
matched, and the coarse SUB-grid fallback answered.

Effect: `get_intra_edge_filter_type` (`reconintra.c`) 0 where libaom has 1, which flips
`av1_use_intra_edge_upsample(8, 4, -6, type)` (`blk_wh == 12`: false at type 1, TRUE at type 0).
We upsampled an edge libaom leaves alone.

Ladder (chroma 8x4 at (688,100) = mi (51,344), V_PRED `p_angle=84`, `ft=1` in aomdec):
aomdec `EC_PREDOUT sum=15924`, ours `sum=15930`; per-sample +1 at rows 2/3, cols 1,2,4.
Hand-simulating both walks from the real above row (A[-1..7] = 495,495,496,497,498,498,499,499,499,
`dx = dr_intra_derivative(84) = 7`) reproduces 15924 unfiltered/un-upsampled and 15930 through
`av1_upsample_intra_edge_high` -- the upsample flag was the whole difference.

EVIDENCE: $HOME/.cache/kf900/pred_aom.txt + pred_ours.txt | EC_PRED on aomdec and decode_probe
over hg_kf900.obu | EC_PREDOUT 15924 vs OUR_PRED 15930; after the fix the block is 0-diff and
the frame's first chroma difference moves (102,688) -> (195,640)

Not the cause (ruled out this round): per-plane quantizer deltas (`EC_AV1_TRACE` on the frame:
`base_q_idx=35 y_dc=0 u_dc=0 u_ac=0 v_dc=0 v_ac=0`, and `plane_q_delta()` is applied at every
chroma dequant call site anyway).

Sibling gates re-run after the fix (`chroma` + `skipped_8x8` filters): 15 passed, 0 failed --
includes `..._chroma_edge_filter_reads_a_sub16_neighbours_uv_mode...`,
`..._directional_chroma...`, `..._smooth_paeth_chroma...`, `..._4to1_or_sub8_rect...`,
`the_chroma_rect_gates_excluded_seed_46...`.

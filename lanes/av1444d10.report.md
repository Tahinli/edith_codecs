# lane-av1444d10 — the 10-bit 4:4:4 352x242 chroma divergence is a chroma PALETTE PREDICTION window, fixed, and gated byte-exact

**Status: FIXED and GATED.** The census cell `s444_352x242_10b` is byte-exact on all
17 decode-order frames against the instrumented aomdec, and so are all 106 committed
fixtures except the nine 4:2:2 ones that refuse by name. Two commits:
`f3afa5b7` (the fix) and `eb73ef89` (the fixture + the byte-exact gate + the
reachability counter). Base `ed7c99bc`.

The census was right that this cell diverged and right that it was outside the 4:2:2
question. It was not, however, "geometry-sensitive" in the way its own framing
implies: **352x242's distinction is not frame width or height at all — it is the
number of CHROMA TRANSFORM UNITS in one block.**

## 2. PREDICTION or RESIDUAL: the filter chain is exonerated, and it is a PREDICTION defect

The brief asked me to check the frame header before assuming the filter chain is
inert. It is inert here, and the check does not need the header: **the oracle's own
pre-filter dump equals the oracle's own final dump.**

| oracle rung | what it is | frame-0 size | vs FINAL (low byte), every sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EC_AV1_PREFILT_DUMP` | after tile decode, before ANY loop filter, `p[c] & 0xFF` | 255 552 B (352x242x3, 1 B/sample) | **0 differences** |
| `EC_AV1_FINAL_DUMP` | after CDEF + superres + LR, u16 LE | 511 104 B | (the reference) |

Both rungs narrow to the same projection (`narrow[c] = p[c] & 0xFF`, per the
rung's own `ec_dump_narrow_row`), so the comparison is apples to apples: **deblock,
CDEF, loop restoration and superres changed no low byte anywhere in this cell.**

Ours, same way: our `EC_AV1_PREFILT_DUMP` (352x256 aligned, 1 B/sample) against the
oracle's over the crop region has **exactly 104 differences — U row 224 cols 64..116
(53) and V row 224 cols 64..114 (51)** — i.e. the *same set* as our FINAL diff set,
and not one more. The defect is therefore pre-filter: **reconstruction**, and inside
reconstruction, **prediction, not residual**:

| stage | measured | what it rules out |
|---|---|---|
| entropy decode | in lockstep: 17/17 frames on both sides, no desync, byte-identical bitstream parse | syntax / mode / coefficient read |
| residual (probe) | `res0 = [0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0]`, `rescol0 = [0,…]` on the owning unit; the residual is read identically on both sides | inverse transform, dequant |
| **prediction (probe)** | **the owning unit's prediction buffer is wrong**: see §3 | — |
| deblock / CDEF / LR / superres | oracle PREFILT == oracle FINAL, low byte, every sample | all four |

`EC_PRED=1` on our side (the `OUR_PRED` / `OUR_RECON` rungs, `decode.rs:4650`) prints,
for the owning unit:

```text
OUR_PRED x=64 y=224 plane=1 side=32 mode=0 sum=442144 row0=[209 x32] col0=[209,216,216,…]
```

against the oracle's, which for that block prints **nothing at all** — because the
oracle's `EC_PREDND` only fires for non-directional predictions and a palette block
returns before it. Aligning the two traces by `(plane, txw, txh, sum)` (the oracle's
`mi_row`/`mi_col` in that print are not pixel/8, so coordinates do not align) the
sides agree on every event until this one, where ours has TWO extra
`plane=1 txw=32 txh=32 mode=0` events the oracle does not have.

## 3. The owning block, and the named expression

**Block.** A 32x64 luma strip at pixel (64, 192) — the oracle's own rung says
`plane=0 row_off=0 col_off=0 txw=32 txh=64`. At `ss_x = ss_y = 0` its chroma plane
block is **also 32x64**, which `decode_block_rect` tiles into `nh = 2` units of 32x32
at pixel (64, 192) and **(64, 224)**.

**Chroma mode: PALETTE.** `EC_DEBUG_PAL=1` shows the unit at (64, 224) consuming a
2048-entry (= 32x64, the whole plane block) palette prediction buffer handed over by
`set_palette_pred` at `decode.rs:20086`, with `stale=false`. `EC_PRED=1` on our side
confirms the `pred: Option<Vec<u16>>` override was taken (`pred_opt=true`) while the
neighbouring block at (96, 224) took the ordinary `PAETH_PRED` path
(`pred_opt=false`).

**THE EXPRESSION — ours vs libaom.**

| | libaom | ours (before) |
|---|---|---|
| where a palette chroma unit's prediction comes from | `av1_visit_palette` → `av1_predict_intra_block_palette` (`reconintra.c:1707`) hands `av1_predict_intra_block` a `ref` pointer at the unit's own origin, and `read_coeffs` walks the plane block with each unit's own `blk_row`/`blk_col` | `FrameCtx::palette_pred` (thread-local `Option<Vec<u16>>`), `decode.rs:20086`, handed **the whole block buffer** |
| the read | the unit's pixels are the unit's | `PlaneBuf::reconstruct` (`decode.rs:21336`) does `prediction[idx]` for `idx in 0..side*side` (write loop at `decode.rs:21406-21433`) — **the first `side*side` entries, i.e. always the buffer's top-left corner** |

So on the 32-capped tiled chroma walk, unit (0, 1) at pixel (64, 224) reconstructed
**the block's row-192 palette values** (flat 209) where libaom writes **row 224's**
(216). Both arms were wrong: the `uw == uh` square-unit arm (`decode.rs:20086`) and
the rect-unit arm (`decode.rs:20140`) — both line numbers PRE-fix, quoted as they stood
at `ed7c99bc`.

`palette_window` already existed for exactly this (`decode.rs:2968-2974`: "A unit reads
its prediction at ITS OWN stride, so handing it the whole-block buffer makes every
unit reconstruct the block's top-left corner … the luma split-transform paths already
window; the chroma ones did not"). Its doc comment records the 4x4 **lossless** chroma
instance as fixed. The 32-capped 4:4:4 instance was still un-windowed: the same
defect, a second walk.

**Why 53 wrong columns and not 32 — the halo is the same bug, not a second one.**
Unit (0,1) writes 209 at col 95, row 224. The neighbouring 32x32 `PAETH_PRED` block at
(96, 224) has that pixel as its left edge, and `EC_DEBUG_EDGES=1` prints its edges as

```text
above=[216 x18, 215, 220, 228, 185, 95, 49, 59, 63, 63, 64, 64, 65, 64, 63]
left =[209, 216, 216, …]            corner=216
```

`paeth(above, left[0], corner)` with `left[0]=209` returns `left` exactly where
`|above − corner| <= |above + left − 2·corner|`, i.e. for `above ∈ {216, 215}` — 19
columns — and returns `above` for `above ∈ {220, 228}` (the two columns where it is
wrong for the other reason) and from col 117 on. That reproduces our observed row
`[209 x19, 216, 216, 185, 95, …]` term for term, and the 53 = 32 + 19 + 2 span the
census measured. **One defect, one fix, both symptoms.** I did not chase the halo as a
second bug, and §4 shows the fix removes it.

**The fix** (post-commit `decode.rs:20106-20113` and `:20164-20172`):

```rust
let pbuf = if plane_idx == 1 { ub } else { vb };
set_palette_pred(
    palette_window(pbuf, chroma_w, cu_col * uw, cu_row * uh, uw, uh),
    fctx,
);
```

## 4. The geometry question, answered

The brief asked what 352x242 has that 320x242 / 352x250 / 416x242 / 416x250 do not.
Measured, it is **not** a partial column at a block or tile boundary, not a plane
extent landing on a 4:4:4 granularity edge, and not a sub-8x8 leaf at the right edge.
It is:

> **a block whose CHROMA is taller than the 32-pixel unit cap at `ss (0,0)`, so
> `decode_block_rect`'s chroma walk has `nh = 2` and every unit after the first is
> handed the wrong window.**

`352 = 5.5 superblocks at 64` and `242` odd were the census's tells, and both are real
facts about the frame — but neither is the discriminator, which is why the census could
only say "geometry-sensitive". The 416x250 cell has an odd height too and is exact;
the 352x250 cell has the same width and is exact; 320x242 has the same odd height and
is exact. What separates 352x242 is that its partition happens to contain a 32x64
chroma plane block with a UV palette.


Four non-vacuity arms, all required to pass:

1. the pinned bytes — length 24 875 and FNV-1a64 `0x7fda_dbb4_3bbf_9af3` (a missing
   file is a **failure**, not a skip);
2. 16 shown frames at 352x242 with `f.u.len() == 352*242` — full-resolution chroma, so
   a decoder that kept the 4:2:0 extent cannot pass the byte compare;
3. `chroma_palette_window_hits()` read as a **delta** with a **measured floor of 4**
   (two tiled palette chroma blocks x U and V). It is incremented on the two fixed
   lines and nowhere else, so a gate that passed without them running would be
   gate-blind-to-feature, not byte-exact;
4. the oracle comparator has its own per-plane one-byte-flip liveness control in the
   crate (`every_oracle_comparator_reds_on_a_one_byte_wrong_oracle`), and my own
   three-plane flip run above.

## 8. What I did NOT do, and what a reviewer should attack

* **Not a superblock/tile-boundary, odd-dimension or sub-8 defect.** I checked the
  three hypotheses the brief named and refuted all three on the controls: same-depth
  same-width (352x250), same-depth same-height (320x242, 416x242) and wider 4:4:4 at
  the same odd height are all exact. The discriminator is chroma unit count.
* **No filter stage involved**, and that is measured, not assumed (§2).
* **Not the 4:2:0 group-tail chroma SKIP arm** — different format, different write
  path, and that arm is left exactly as it was.
* **The 4:2:2 sequence-header guard was not touched.** Both diffs are
  `decode.rs` (the two `palette_window` arms + the counter) and `stream.rs` (the gate),
  plus one new binary fixture.
* **The mutation proof is the strongest single check here and it is honest**: the
  windowing was removed while the counter stayed, so the byte compare — not the
  reachability assert — is what reddened.
* **What a reviewer should attack first.** The counter's floor of 4 is measured on this
  one stream. It is a *reachability* floor, not a census: a second palette-chroma
  fixture would move it, and the gate would then need its floor re-measured. The
  byte-exactness arm is the one that carries the exactness claim and it does not
  depend on the number.
* **Not swept beyond the one walk.** I audited every `set_palette_pred` call site in
  `decode.rs` (~100). All the others either pass a whole-block buffer to exactly ONE
  push (correct by construction) or already window with `palette_window`. The two I
  changed were the only whole-buffer hand-offs inside a tiled unit loop. I did not
  build a 4:4:4 12-bit or a `nw > 1` variant to prove the `nw > 1` arm; on this
  stream `nw == 1` always and the horizontal windowing is unmeasured.
